# How to Measure Flour Accurately

- **Category:** General baking techniques; measuring ingredients; troubleshooting
- **Source:** King Arthur Baking, “How to measure flour the right way”
- **Author:** Rossi Anastopoulo
- **Published:** October 13, 2023
- **Original URL:** https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/blog/2023/10/13/how-to-measure-flour

## Why does accurate flour measurement matter?

An excess of flour can make baked goods dry or crumbly. This can affect bread and cookies, among other recipes. Flour measured by volume is inconsistent because the amount depends on how tightly the flour is packed into a cup. Weighing ingredients is more reliable than using measuring cups.

For King Arthur Baking's all-purpose flour and recipes, **1 cup corresponds to 120 g**. A tightly packed cup can contain as much as **160 g**, adding substantially more flour than intended. Use the flour weight specified in your particular recipe when available; not every recipe developer defines a cup the same way.

## How to measure flour with a kitchen scale (preferred method)

1. Turn on the scale and set the unit to grams.
2. Place the empty bowl or container on the scale.
3. Press **Tare** to reset the display to zero with the container on it.
4. Add flour until the display reaches the weight required by the recipe.
5. If following a King Arthur recipe calling for 1 cup of all-purpose flour, weigh **120 g**.

The source notes that a difference of about **5 g above or below** the specified amount will generally not change the result significantly, but recommends measuring as accurately as possible.

## How to measure flour with measuring cups (when no scale is available)

Use the **fluff, sprinkle, and scrape** method:

1. **Fluff:** Stir the flour gently in its bag or container with a spoon to loosen it.
2. **Sprinkle:** Spoon the loosened flour into the measuring cup until it overflows. Avoid compressing or packing it down.
3. **Scrape:** Sweep a straight-edged knife across the top to level the cup without pressing the flour.

This helps avoid the extra flour that results from packing a measuring cup too tightly.

## Troubleshooting common baking problems

### Why did my bread turn out dry?

One possible cause is adding more flour than the recipe intended. Check whether you packed the measuring cup or scooped up compacted flour. Next time, weigh the flour directly according to the recipe.

### Why are my cookies crumbly or dry?

Excess flour may have made the dough too dry. Measure with a scale, or use the fluff, sprinkle, and scrape technique when measuring by volume.

### Is 1 cup of flour always 120 g?

No. **120 g is King Arthur Baking's standard for a cup of its all-purpose flour.** Follow the recipe's listed gram weight when provided; weights can differ across types of flour and recipe developers.

### What happens if I scoop flour directly from the bag with the measuring cup?

The flour may pack together and you may add too much. A tightly packed cup of all-purpose flour can weigh up to 160 g instead of the intended 120 g in the King Arthur method.

### Do I need a scale to bake successfully?

A scale is recommended for consistency, but measuring cups can still work if flour is fluffed, spooned into the cup, and leveled carefully.

## Key takeaway

For consistent baking, weigh flour using the amount specified in the recipe. If using volume measurements, loosen the flour, spoon it into the cup, and level without packing. When troubleshooting dry bread or crumbly cookies, check flour measurement first.
