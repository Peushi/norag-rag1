# Why Are My Cookies Spreading? Troubleshooting Guide

Source: https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/blog/2023/12/19/why-are-my-cookies-spreading
Author: Annabelle Shippee, King Arthur Baking
Published: 2023-12-19
Topic: Cookies — excessive spreading during baking

## Problem: Cookies spread too much

Cookies can bake into thin, flat, greasy, or misshapen rounds instead of keeping their intended size or shape. Both the dough's ingredients and the baking environment can contribute.

## Common causes and what to check

### 1. Ingredient proportions are inaccurate
Too much liquid or sugar compared with dry ingredients encourages spreading. Weigh ingredients with a kitchen scale and follow the recipe's specified proportions.

### 2. Eggs are larger than the recipe specifies
Oversized eggs add extra moisture. Use the egg size listed in the recipe. A typical large egg contains roughly 50 g of egg without its shell.

### 3. Butter is too warm
Very soft or partially melted butter allows cookies to spread before their structure sets. When a recipe asks for room-temperature butter, it should still feel cool and resist slightly when pressed, while being soft enough to indent.

### 4. Baking powder or baking soda has lost effectiveness
Old leaveners may not give cookies the intended lift. Check their freshness and replace them when necessary.

### 5. Oven temperature is too low
An oven may indicate that it is preheated before it has actually reached the selected temperature. In an oven that is too cool, fat may melt before the cookie sets. Confirm the temperature with an oven thermometer.

## Ways to reduce cookie spreading

### Chill the dough
Refrigerate dough for about 30–60 minutes if compatible with the recipe, then bake while it is still chilled. This keeps butter firm for longer and gives flour time to absorb moisture.

### Consider the fat used
Shortening melts at a higher temperature than butter and can reduce spreading. In recipes where a substitution is appropriate, replace some or all of the butter with shortening by volume; using half butter and half shortening balances flavor and structure. Do not automatically change recipes in which butter is central to the result.

### Use cooled browned butter where appropriate
Browning removes some water from butter, which may help cookies keep their shape. Cool it before mixing, and measure the amount required by weight after browning. Browned butter changes the flavor and adds brown flecks, so it is not ideal for every cookie style.

### Slightly reduce sugar if a recipe consistently spreads
If other causes have been ruled out, try reducing the sugar by around 10% **by weight**. Less sugar can make dough firmer by allowing flour to absorb more moisture. This is an adjustment to test, not a universal instruction for every recipe.

### Line baking sheets instead of greasing them
Use parchment paper or a silicone baking mat. Greasing the sheet adds extra fat and can increase spreading.

### Cool trays between batches
Do not place fresh dough on a hot baking sheet. Allow the tray to cool completely so the dough does not begin melting before entering the oven.

## Quick troubleshooting questions

**Why did my cookies turn out flat and thin?** Check ingredient ratios, butter temperature, the freshness of baking soda or baking powder, and actual oven temperature.

**Will chilling cookie dough help?** Often yes. Chilling for 30–60 minutes can slow fat melting and limit spreading.

**Should I grease the cookie sheet?** Prefer parchment paper or a silicone mat rather than a greased surface.

**Why does my second batch spread more than the first?** You may have placed the dough on a still-hot baking sheet. Cool the pan first.

**Can I reduce sugar to stop cookies spreading?** If a recipe repeatedly spreads despite correct technique, try reducing sugar by about 10% by weight and compare the result.

**Do I need different eggs?** Use the size specified by the recipe; larger eggs introduce more moisture.
