# Brownie Texture: Fudgy, Cakey, and Chewy

Publisher: King Arthur Baking

## What makes brownie textures different?

Brownies can be fudgy, cakey, or chewy even when recipes use similar ingredients. The key differences are the proportions of flour, fat, sugar, eggs, liquid, and leavening. Compare the full recipe rather than relying on a single ingredient.

## Fudgy brownies

**Texture:** Dense, moist, rich, and compact rather than airy.

**Typical recipe features:** Relatively little flour and fewer eggs; plenty of fat, often oil or melted butter; little or no extra liquid or baking powder. Cocoa-based formulations are common, although melted chocolate can also produce fudgy brownies.

**What to try:** Choose a recipe designed for fudgy brownies. Avoid increasing the flour or adding extra baking powder in the hope of improving texture.

## Cakey brownies

**Texture:** Lighter, airier, and more cake-like, while still moist.

**Typical recipe features:** More flour and eggs relative to other ingredients, often some water or coffee and baking powder for rise. Butter is commonly used.

**What to try:** Choose a cakey brownie recipe with sufficient structure and leavening rather than trying to modify a fudgy recipe at random.

## Chewy brownies

**Texture:** Dense and pleasantly resistant to the bite, between fudgy and cakey.

**Typical recipe features:** A comparatively high proportion of sugar; oil or melted butter; often cocoa powder rather than melted chocolate; generally no extra liquid or leavening.

**What to try:** Use a recipe developed for chewiness, rather than assuming that longer baking alone creates a chewy brownie.

## How individual ingredients affect texture

- **Flour:** More flour generally promotes a cakier, more structured crumb; less flour makes brownies more compact and fudgy.
- **Butter and oil:** More fat makes brownies richer. Oil contributes moisture because it is liquid at room temperature; butter contributes flavor. Melted butter is frequently used for fudgy textures.
- **Sugar:** Helps retain moisture and softness. High-sugar recipes can also have a chewier texture. Partly dissolving sugar during preparation can help create a shiny, crackly surface.
- **Cocoa powder and melted chocolate:** Cocoa powder absorbs liquid and can dry a brownie without enough fat. Melted chocolate can support a rich, fudgy result. Cocoa powder has concentrated chocolate flavor.
- **Eggs:** Provide protein, fat, moisture, and structure. Higher egg ratios may make brownies cakier.
- **Baking powder:** Adds lift and a more airy crumb; little or no leavener helps maintain a dense texture.
- **Water or coffee:** Additional liquid can support a cakier crumb. Coffee can intensify perceived chocolate flavor.

## Troubleshooting common texture questions

### Why are my brownies too cakey?

The recipe may use comparatively more flour, eggs, extra liquid, or baking powder. If you wanted dense brownies, start with a fudgy or chewy recipe instead of changing baking time alone.

### Why are my brownies dense instead of fluffy?

Dense brownies may be the intended result of lower flour, little or no baking powder, and a high proportion of fat or sugar. For a lighter result, choose a specifically cakey brownie formula.

### How do I get a shiny, crackly brownie top?

Some brownie recipes create a thin, shiny top by partially dissolving sugar before baking. The source also notes that chocolate chips can help produce a shiny crust in its recipes; follow the recipe's mixing method and ingredient recommendations.

### Does using oil instead of butter change brownies?

Yes. Oil usually gives a moister impression; butter gives a distinct buttery flavor. Replacements can change texture, so follow a tested recipe if the result matters.

## Understanding baker's percentages

A baker's percentage compares an ingredient's weight with flour weight. Flour is always 100%. For example, if a recipe contains 200 g flour and 250 g sugar, sugar is (250 / 200) × 100 = 125%. This helps compare ingredient ratios among brownie recipes without confusing weight and volume.
