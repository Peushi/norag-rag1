# Sourdough Troubleshooting: Frequently Asked Questions

Source: King Arthur Baking — Sourdough Baking FAQ  
Original URL: https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/learn/guides/sourdough/faq  
Document type: Practical FAQ summary for a baking troubleshooting knowledge base

## My sourdough starter has been neglected. How can I revive it?

**Symptoms:** A refrigerated starter has not been fed for a long time, has liquid on top, or produces few bubbles.

**Steps:**
1. If the starter has no signs of spoilage, stir the liquid back in.
2. Keep 113 g (approximately 1/2 cup) of starter; discard the remainder.
3. Add 113 g water and 113 g flour and mix.
4. Watch for bubbling over the following hours. Once it becomes active, keep 113 g and feed with another 113 g water and 113 g flour.
5. If it expands and bubbles within 12 hours, give it one more feeding before baking or refrigerating it.

**When to discard:** Throw the starter away if you see mold, pink or orange coloring, or an unusual odor other than its normal sour smell. An alcoholic smell alone can occur in a hungry starter.

## Why is my sourdough not rising? What does gluten do?

Flour proteins gliadin and glutenin form gluten when mixed with water and worked into a dough. Gliadin contributes stretch; glutenin contributes elasticity. Kneading helps build a network that can trap carbon dioxide produced during fermentation. This trapped gas enables the dough to expand. A weak gluten structure can make it harder for dough to hold its rise.

## Why is one sourdough loaf more sour than another?

Flavor depends on the recipe and fermentation conditions, not just the starter. Longer fermentation can develop more flavor. A cold overnight rise generally produces a more pronounced sour flavor than a shorter room-temperature rise, partly because the balance of fermentation acids changes. Follow your recipe's fermentation guidance to avoid going too far.

## Should I add pineapple juice, honey, grapes, or other ingredients to a starter?

A basic sourdough starter needs flour and water: flour supplies food for the microorganisms. Adding honey, grapes, potato, or similar ingredients is not necessary to feed it. Some bakers use pineapple juice because of its acidity, but the source FAQ does not establish that it works better than water.

## What does each ingredient do in sourdough bread?

- **Flour:** Supplies starches for fermentation and proteins that form gluten, helping dough retain gas.
- **Water:** Hydrates flour, helps gluten form, supports fermentation, and disperses other ingredients.
- **Wild yeast in the starter:** Produces carbon dioxide that helps dough rise and contributes fermentation byproducts to flavor.
- **Salt:** Adds flavor and slows fermentation; it also affects the final dough and crust.
- **Sugar:** Flour can supply fermentable sugars naturally, so adding sugar is not required just to feed the yeast.
- **Fat (oil or butter):** Can make the crumb softer and richer but is not essential in basic sourdough bread.

## Quick troubleshooting questions

**My starter looks flat after weeks in the fridge. Is it dead?** Not necessarily. If there is no spoilage, try the repeated feedings above and look for bubbles and expansion.

**My starter has pink spots or mold. Can I scrape them off?** No. Discard the starter.

**How can I make sourdough taste more sour?** A longer, cool fermentation can help, subject to the recipe and dough condition.

**Do I need honey to activate sourdough?** No. Flour provides the material needed for fermentation.

**Why doesn't dough retain the gas from fermentation?** Gluten structure helps trap gas. Dough made without sufficient gluten development may struggle to retain its rise.
