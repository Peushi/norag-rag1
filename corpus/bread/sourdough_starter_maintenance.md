# Sourdough Starter Maintenance and Feeding

**Category:** Bread / sourdough  
**Topic:** Starter feeding, storage, revival, expansion, and discard  
**Source:** King Arthur Baking, *Sourdough Baking — Maintain*  
**URL:** https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/learn/guides/sourdough/maintain

## How often should I feed a sourdough starter?

The schedule depends on storage temperature and how often you bake. A starter kept at room temperature needs feeding roughly every 12 hours (twice daily). A refrigerated starter can be maintained with a feeding about once a week. Infrequent feeding may make a starter slower to become active when you want to bake.

## Standard maintenance feeding: How much starter, flour, and water?

Keep **113 g of existing starter** and remove the remainder. Mix in **113 g of water** and **113 g of flour**. This is a 1:1:1 feeding by weight, resulting in about 339 g of refreshed starter. A kitchen scale is more reliable than cups because starter volume changes with its thickness and how many bubbles it contains.

## How do I store and feed starter in the refrigerator?

1. About once a week, retain 113 g of starter and set the rest aside for discard recipes or disposal.
2. Add 113 g water and 113 g flour; mix and cover.
3. Leave at room temperature until some bubbles appear, typically **1–2 hours**.
4. Return the covered starter to the refrigerator.

## How do I maintain starter at room temperature?

Feed about **every 12 hours**. At each feeding, retain 113 g starter and add 113 g each of flour and water. Room-temperature starter ferments faster than chilled starter and therefore requires more frequent feeding.

## I forgot to feed my starter. Can I revive it?

A neglected starter may be sluggish but can often recover. Feed it at approximately 12-hour intervals until it becomes healthy and active again, then resume its usual schedule. A starter left unused for months can require a substantial recovery period. The timing is not exact: activity varies with starter condition, flour, hydration, and ambient temperature.

If you see mold or unusual pink or orange discoloration, do not try to revive the starter; discard it. For detailed spoilage guidance, consult the separate sourdough FAQ document.

## How do I build up more starter for a recipe?

If a recipe needs more than **227 g** of starter, increase the amount over successive feedings **without discarding**, while reserving at least 113 g for future maintenance.

Example from the source:

1. Start with 113 g starter and add 113 g water plus 113 g flour: approximately 339 g starter.
2. At the next feeding, keep all 339 g and add 339 g water plus 339 g flour: approximately 1,017 g starter.

Always plan enough for both the recipe and the starter you want to keep.

## Can I use whole wheat or rye flour to feed my starter?

Yes. Whole-grain wheat and rye flour can promote activity, especially when establishing a starter. Once the starter is mature, ordinary all-purpose flour is adequate for routine feedings. For a whole-grain bread, a final feeding with whole-grain flour may increase activity; keep a separate portion of your regular starter if desired.

## What should I do with sourdough discard?

- **Cook or bake with it:** Use a recipe developed for sourdough discard.
- **Compost it:** Add excess starter to a compost bin.
- **Throw it away:** Spread the discard on parchment or waxed paper, let it dry or bake it until solid, then dispose of it.
- **Avoid pouring thick discard down the sink:** It may clog plumbing. The source describes heavily diluting it with cold water only when there is no other option.

## Quick troubleshooting questions

**Why is my refrigerated starter not very bubbly?** It may need feeding and time at room temperature to become active again; refrigerated starters ferment slowly.

**How often should I feed starter if I bake every day?** Room-temperature maintenance typically involves feeding every 12 hours.

**How often should I feed starter if I bake occasionally?** Refrigeration with a weekly feeding is a practical option.

**Should I measure starter in cups or grams?** Grams are preferable because starter volume varies considerably.

**What if I need 500 g of sourdough starter?** Increase the starter through one or more feedings without discarding, making enough for the recipe and an additional 113 g to retain.

**Can I use all-purpose flour instead of rye for starter maintenance?** Yes, once the starter is established.

**Does a missed feeding mean the starter is ruined?** Not necessarily; repeated feedings can often revive it, provided there are no signs of spoilage.
