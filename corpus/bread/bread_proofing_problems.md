# Dough Not Rising: Proofing and Fermentation Troubleshooting

Source: King Arthur Baking — “Ask the Bread Coach: My dough isn’t rising — what now?”
Author: David Tamarkin (interview with bread coach Martin Philip)
Published: 2023-03-23
URL: https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/blog/2023/03/23/ask-the-bread-coach-my-dough-isnt-rising-what-now

## Problem: My bread dough is rising too slowly

Slow fermentation can produce dense, short loaves if dough is baked before it has risen sufficiently. In a cold home, the dough may simply need more time. The article's central advice is to check measurements and temperature before trying shortcuts.

## First checks: ingredient amounts, yeast, and temperature

- Measure ingredients carefully, including salt and yeast.
- Measure the temperature of the mixing water and the dough with a thermometer.
- Check that the yeast is appropriate for the recipe, or that the sourdough starter is healthy and active.
- Check room temperature. A cold room can slow down otherwise healthy dough.

### What dough temperature should I aim for?

The bread coach gives a general target dough temperature of **75–80°F (about 24–27°C)** for good fermentation. Individual recipes may specify a different target. A room around **70–72°F (about 21–22°C)** may work if the dough starts in the target range, but a colder room will not warm up cool dough.

## Fix 1: Give cold dough more time

If the dough is cool and rising slowly, extend bulk fermentation rather than following the recipe's timing rigidly. The interview suggests extending fermentation by approximately **25%–50%** when necessary. Check the dough's actual progress instead of relying only on the clock.

## Fix 2: Make a safely warmer proofing environment

- **Proofing box:** A proofing environment around **80–85°F (27–29°C)** can help sluggish dough rise faster.
- **Microwave as a warm enclosure (not running with dough inside):** Heat a half-filled jar of water in the microwave for about two minutes, then place the dough in the switched-off microwave next to the warm jar.
- **Warm-water bath:** Place a sealed, watertight container of dough inside a larger bowl with warm water around **105–120°F (41–49°C)**. This figure is the *water-bath temperature*, not the desired dough temperature.
- **Heating pad or seed-starting mat:** Gentle external warmth can help; monitor the dough so it does not get too warm.
- **Naturally warm place:** Look for a modestly warm location in the house, and check its temperature.

### Can I proof dough in an oven with the light on?

The interview advises caution: someone could accidentally switch on the oven while dough is inside. Other warming options may be safer. Avoid excessive heat, regardless of method.

## Problem: How do I avoid overproofing when warming dough?

Check the dough frequently as it rises. Higher temperatures can speed fermentation but may also cause the dough to overproof. Maintaining a controlled temperature and observing the dough matters more than using maximum heat.

## Problem: My dough has barely risen after an hour. Should I knead in more yeast?

The bread coach does **not** recommend extra yeast as the first fix. Adding yeast late complicates the dough. Prefer to extend fermentation and observe its progress. If the loaf does not turn out well, use the experience to adjust the next batch.

## Planning ahead for a cold kitchen

When a kitchen is consistently cold, the interview suggests starting with a slightly warmer dough, around **82–83°F (28–28.5°C)**, anticipating that it will cool. This is a cold-kitchen workaround, not the standard target for every dough. Adjust the initial water temperature carefully and monitor fermentation.

## Quick question-and-answer recap

**Q: Why is my dough not rising in a cold room?**  
A: Low dough and room temperatures slow fermentation. Check yeast or starter health, measure the dough temperature, then provide gentle warmth or additional time.

**Q: Do I need to throw away slow-rising dough?**  
A: Not necessarily. Give it longer to ferment and observe it before deciding to bake.

**Q: What temperature is best for bread dough?**  
A: A commonly recommended starting target is 75–80°F (24–27°C), unless the recipe says otherwise.

**Q: How much extra proofing time might cold dough need?**  
A: The article suggests increasing bulk fermentation time by roughly 25%–50%, depending on conditions and dough progress.

**Q: Is warmer always better?**  
A: No. Excessive warmth risks overproofing. Check the dough during fermentation.
