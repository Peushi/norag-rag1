# Baking Sourdough Bread: Starter Readiness and Step-by-Step Guide

**Category:** Bread / Sourdough  
**Source:** King Arthur Baking, *Sourdough Baking — Bake*  
**Original URL:** https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/learn/guides/sourdough/bake  
**Source type:** Baking guide (no article publication date specified in the provided page)

## What is this guide for?

This guide describes a naturally leavened sourdough bread that rises using active sourdough starter rather than packaged yeast. It makes two round loaves. Beginners whose starters are not reliably active may prefer a recipe that also includes commercial yeast.

## How can I tell if sourdough starter is ready to bake?

A maintained starter is not necessarily active enough to raise bread. Feed it until it can **double in volume within 6–8 hours**, looks lively and bubbly, and has a clean, sharp smell. A ready starter has bubbles on the surface and a somewhat elastic feel. A slightly sunken top with many foamy bubbles suggests it has just passed its peak, but it may still work. Set aside **113 g** of starter for future feedings; feed the retained portion before refrigerating it.

## Ingredients for two loaves

- 454 g active, fed sourdough starter
- 602 g all-purpose flour
- 85 g whole wheat or white whole wheat flour
- 397 g room-temperature water
- 2 1/2 teaspoons salt

Measure starter by weight if possible. If measuring by volume, stir it down before measuring so trapped air does not distort the amount.

## How to bake the sourdough bread

### Step 1 — Mix the dough

Mix starter, both flours, and water until no dry flour remains and the mixture forms a cohesive, soft, slightly sticky dough. **Do not add salt yet.** Adjust the water or flour slightly only if needed to reach the intended consistency.

### Step 2 — Rest the dough (autolyse)

Cover and leave the dough for **20 minutes**. This pause hydrates the flour and starts gluten development, helping the dough become easier to knead.

### Step 3 — Add salt, knead, and rise

Mix in the salt and knead until the dough is smooth, flexible, and still a little tacky. Cover the dough in its bowl and let it rise for **1 hour**.

### Step 4 — Fold and rise again

Turn the dough onto a lightly floured surface. Fold it in thirds like a letter, rotate it **90 degrees**, then repeat. Return it to the bowl, cover, and rest for **another 1 hour**.

### Step 5 — Divide and pre-shape

Divide the dough into **two portions**, gently round each one, cover, and rest for **20 minutes**. The rest relaxes the gluten so final shaping is easier.

### Step 6 — Final shape and proof

Form two firm, round loaves (boules). Put them **seam-side up** in cloth-lined, flour-dusted bowls or on suitable proofing cloth. Cover and allow them to become light and airy, approximately **2–2 1/2 hours**. Use their appearance, not only the clock, to judge proofing.

### Step 7 — Preheat the oven

About **60 minutes before baking**, preheat to **450°F (about 232°C)**. A baking stone is optional. For steam, an empty cast-iron pan can be preheated on a lower rack; handle all hot equipment cautiously.

### Step 8 — Turn out and score

Turn the loaves onto parchment, with the seam underneath. Cut shallow slashes in the tops using a sharp blade. Scoring creates a controlled place for the dough to expand in the oven and can reduce unwanted side splits.

### Step 9 — Add steam carefully

Transfer the loaves to the hot stone or a baking sheet. If using the preheated cast-iron pan method, add **1 cup of boiling water** to the pan to create steam. **Steam and the pan can cause serious burns**: wear suitable oven mitts and keep hands and face away from escaping steam.

### Step 10 — Bake and cool

Bake approximately **35–40 minutes**, until the crust is deeply golden and crisp. Transfer the loaves to a cooling rack and cool before slicing.

## Common sourdough baking questions

### Why isn't my sourdough rising?

First check starter activity: a baking-ready starter should double within around 6–8 hours after feeding. Rise also varies with dough temperature and proofing conditions; a sluggish starter may need more feedings before use.

### Why does my bread split on the side?

Without scoring, expanding dough may rupture at an unintended location. Slash the top immediately before baking so expansion has a preferred route.

### Why is my sourdough crust less crisp than expected?

An adequately preheated oven and careful use of steam can support expansion and crust development. Allow the bread to bake until well browned and cool it on a rack.

### Do I have to use a baking stone?

No. The source also permits baking the shaped loaves on a parchment-lined or lightly greased baking sheet.

### What is autolyse?

In this guide, autolyse means resting the mixture of starter, flour, and water for 20 minutes **before adding salt**, so the flour hydrates and the dough becomes easier to work with.

### Can I use starter directly from the refrigerator?

The important test is whether it is sufficiently active. Feed it and check for vigorous bubbling and doubling within 6–8 hours before attempting a naturally leavened loaf.

## Key timings at a glance

| Stage | Approximate time |
| --- | --- |
| Starter readiness after feeding | 6–8 hours to double |
| Autolyse | 20 minutes |
| First rise | 1 hour |
| Rise after folding | 1 hour |
| Rest after pre-shaping | 20 minutes |
| Final proof | 2–2 1/2 hours |
| Oven preheat | About 60 minutes before baking |
| Baking | 35–40 minutes at 450°F / about 232°C |

**Note:** These timings describe the particular King Arthur Baking guide and are not universal rules for all sourdough recipes. Follow changes in the dough as well as elapsed time.
